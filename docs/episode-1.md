At the age of twenty, forgoing higher education, I accepted a position as a security guard at a bustling home furnishings and building supplies mall.
My days there were often filled with lighthearted moments, particularly when engaging in playful banter with the young women in the office and the shop girls.
These exchanges remained friendly yet carried a teasingly ambiguous undertone, characteristic of my youthful innocence.

Among them was one who made my heart move. Later, she was my first.

I joined a tour group for the journey. When the guide asked if everyone would be willing to pay extra to visit sites along the 38th Parallel, Wenwen, a girl in the group, and I opted out. We left on time, at 8:30. The rain had stopped by the time we landed at Beijing Capital. The connection to Chongqing was meant to go at 15:30 in the afternoon. The guide got us moved to an earlier one. That would have put me home three hours sooner, and I hoped they would call it off. They called it off. The 15:30 came back, and I was glad, though nobody else was. Then the weather over Chongqing turned. They called us to the plane at 15:30. Nothing happened. At 19:30 they called us again, and we took off at 21:20. I stepped off at 23:50. It was late and the terminal was full of people. I looked for her in the crowd.

## Weizhou Island

I have visited Weizhou Island twice, with my first trip taking place in July 2011.

My friend and I traveled by train from Chongqing to Nanning, transferred to another train for Beihai, and finally boarded the Feiyu fast ferry to the island.

Our initial accommodation was a place called "Piglet Bar," which proved to be neither comfortable nor budget-friendly. On our first night, however, we splurged several hundred yuan on a seafood feast, indulging in abalone, mantis shrimp, grouper, and, naturally, plenty of beer.

Seeking better value, we later moved to a local guesthouse run by the village head. The rates were far more reasonable, and the fees for processing our fresh catches were fair. We continued our culinary exploration, particularly savoring abalone served with rich sauce over steamed rice—a dish so delicious it could tempt one to eat several bowls. Not every experiment was a success, though; we tried shark meat, which turned out to be truly unpleasant. Its gritty texture, reminiscent of sand, made it quite unpalatable.
